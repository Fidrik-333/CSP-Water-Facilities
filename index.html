Chapter 01

Genesis, Study Area & Field Methodology

Connecting academic observation with rural community reality in Rebala Village through structured site documentation and resident interaction.

Project Genesis & Need for the Study

Drinking water is fundamental to daily health, domestic sanitation, and rural community resilience. In village settlements, households rely on an interwoven network of public facilities—mineral water plants, elevated storage tanks, public standposts, hand pumps, and groundwater borewells—whose day-to-day accessibility depends on physical upkeep and responsible community stewardship.

Field observations, photographs, and location notes collected across separate village visits remain fragmented without a unified record. This Community Service Project was initiated to observe Rebala Village’s existing water infrastructure firsthand, document facility conditions accurately, foster water-conservation awareness among school students, and organize all verified findings into an enduring digital monograph.

Study Area: Rebala Village

Fieldwork was conducted across residential streets, public utility premises, an educational campus, and administrative offices in Rebala Village, located in Buchireddypalem Mandal, SPSR Nellore District, Andhra Pradesh (within the Rebala Gram Sachivalayam, Swarna Rebala 1 & 2 jurisdiction).

Local Administration
Rebala Gram Sachivalayam (Swarna Rebala 1 & 2)
Primary Study Focus
Public water availability, storage structures, community taps, pumping units, and household access
Water Sources Documented

Evidence Scope
Direct physical observation, resident & student interactions, field photography (no lab testing conducted)

Chapter 06

Findings, Recommendations & Water Stewardship

Separating verified field observations from unconfirmed items, alongside practical community recommendations and national water initiatives referenced in the report.

Verified Through Field Visits

Direct Field Observations

  • Rebala Village relies on complementary water infrastructure: a Government RWSS Mineral Water Plant (₹5 coin-operated 20L dispensing and mobile tanker option), an elevated Overhead Water Storage Tank, indoor/outdoor multi-tap points, a shared washing platform, residential hand pumps, and an outdoor pumping installation.
  • Accessibility varies by locality, making well-maintained neighborhood hand pumps and public standposts important for reducing household collection effort—particularly noted during elderly resident interactions.
  • Proper drainage, clean surroundings around shared washing platforms, and leak-free taps are visibly central to keeping public water points hygienic and usable.
  • Interactive discussions at the local school demonstrated strong student receptiveness to practical conservation habits and safeguarding shared community taps.
Not Claimed Without Evidence

Items Requiring Further Confirmation

  • Laboratory Water Quality: No TDS, pH, bacterial, or chemical potability tests were carried out; water safety cannot be certified from visual inspection alone.
  • Technical Capacities: Exact storage volume of the overhead reservoir (litres), pumping horsepower, borewell depths, and household connection totals were not documented.
  • Approximate Map Pins: While the India Mark II hand-pump location is GPS ground-truth verified (14.520941° N, 79.904295° E), the remaining five facility coordinates remain approximate field-referenced locations (~150–250 m) awaiting standalone device GPS fixes.
  • Household Sample Metrics: Qualitative resident interviews were conducted rather than a statistically quantified household census.
06.A · Actionable Priorities

Documented Recommendations

R1

Public Facility Maintenance

Conduct regular inspections of public standposts, connecting pipelines, valves, and hand pumps to repair leaks promptly and reduce preventable water loss.

R2

Platform Sanitation & Drainage

Keep multi-tap washing platforms, standpost aprons, and surrounding collection areas clean with clear drainage channels to prevent stagnant water accumulation.

R3

Energy & Pumping Reliability

Maintain reliable power and pumping upkeep so elevated storage reservoirs and community water-dispensing points operate consistently.

R4

Water-Quality Transparency

Support periodic, documented water-quality testing at mineral water plants and community outlets so residents have clear assurance of drinking water safety.

R5

Preventive Upkeep & Awareness

Sustain community participation and youth awareness around careful household storage, rainwater conservation, and shared infrastructure care.

Water Conservation & Government Initiatives

Conservation Priorities from the Report: Repairing leaking taps and pipelines, maintaining clean household storage vessels, protecting village ponds and surface water bodies, encouraging rainwater harvesting for groundwater recharge, and strengthening local community participation to counter seasonal rainfall variability.

Conclusion

Closing Synthesis

Safe, accessible drinking water is foundational to rural health, hygiene, and everyday dignity. Through direct fieldwork in Rebala Village—spanning administrative engagement at the Gram Sachivalayam, school awareness sessions, physical inspection of the RWSS mineral water plant, overhead storage reservoir, public taps, multi-tap washing platform, hand pumps, and pumping infrastructure—this Community Service Project connected academic inquiry with real community conditions. By organizing these verified observations and providing a structured digital documentary archive, this platform aims to support responsible water conservation, regular facility maintenance, and lasting awareness across the community.
